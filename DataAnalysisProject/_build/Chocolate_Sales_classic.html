Chocolate Sales Analysis¶

Analyses 1,094 chocolate sales transactions (2022) to find the strongest markets, products and sales people, and to look at monthly trends and how order value relates to volume.

Each step documents the method and why it was chosen, the result it produced, and for charts why that chart type fits and what it reveals.

What it does: Loads the Excel file and prints the first rows, structure, data types and summary statistics in one cell.

Why this method: This is the standard 'first look' at a new dataset - confirming columns, checking that Date is already a true datetime, and revealing scale (Amount averages ~5,652).

Result: 1,094 orders, 6 columns, no missing values; countries/products/people are text, amount and boxes numeric.

Summary of Analysis¶

Objective: Analyse 1,094 chocolate sales orders (2022) to identify the strongest countries, products and sales people, examine monthly trends, and test whether revenue tracks shipment volume.

Data & preparation: 1,094 rows x 6 columns (Sales Person, Country, Product, Date, Amount, Boxes Shipped). No missing values; Date already datetime. A Month key was derived for the trend analysis.

Methods: groupby().sum() for revenue by country/product/person; monthly aggregation for the trend line; a scatter plot and correlation for Amount vs Boxes Shipped; a histogram for order value; bar charts for rankings.

Key findings

  • Total sales 6,183,625 across 177,007 boxes; average order 5,652.31.
  • Country revenue is evenly spread: Australia 1.14M (top) down to New Zealand 0.95M (bottom).
  • Products are close: Smooth Sliky Salty 349,692 (best) to 70% Dark Bites 211,610 (worst).
  • Sales people vary most: Ches Bonnell 320,901 (best) vs Wilone O'Kielt 138,523 (worst) - a 2.3x gap.
  • Monthly sales fluctuate in a narrow band (674k-896k) with no clear trend (peak January, low April).
  • Revenue and boxes shipped are essentially uncorrelated (-0.02).

Conclusions & recommendations

  • The business is geographically and product-wise diversified; no single market or product carries it, which lowers concentration risk.
  • The clearest performance gaps are at the individual-salesperson level, so coaching could focus there.
  • The near-zero Amount/Boxes correlation is suspicious and should be checked with the data owner (possible synthetic data or inconsistent pricing).

Limitations: Only one year (8 months) of data; small per-person samples; the Amount/Boxes anomaly prevents any firm price-volume conclusion.
